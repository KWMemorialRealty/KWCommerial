From first call to closing.

What working with us on office property typically looks like.

Leasing or buying office space

  1. Define your needsHeadcount and growth, layout, location and commute, parking, budget, and when your current lease ends.
  2. Search and tourWe compare options on total cost, building quality, parking and amenities, then tour them with you.

  5. Lease signing or closingWe coordinate documents, due diligence and the lease execution or closing.

Questions

Common questions.

What is the difference between Class A, B and C office space?

Office classes are a shorthand for building quality. Class A buildings are typically the newest or best-located with the highest-quality finishes, Class B are older but well-maintained, and Class C are older buildings that may need updating, usually at lower rents.

Should my business lease or buy office space?

It depends on your growth plans, capital and how long you expect to stay. Buying can build equity for an owner-user; leasing keeps capital free and offers flexibility. An office agent can model both for your situation.

What is the difference between rentable and usable square feet?

Usable square feet is the space your business occupies. Rentable square feet adds your share of common areas such as lobbies and restrooms, and it is usually the figure rent is based on.

What is a tenant improvement allowance?

It is money a landlord provides toward building out or updating a tenant's space. The amount is negotiated and often depends on lease length and the tenant's credit.

What is a full-service lease?

In a full-service, or gross, lease the rent includes most operating costs such as utilities, taxes and maintenance. Increases above a base year are often passed through to the tenant.

When should a business start looking for new office space?

Many businesses start 12 to 18 months before their lease ends, and earlier for larger spaces, to leave time to compare options, negotiate and complete a build-out.
