What to evaluate

Before you buy, sell or lease.

Questions our agents work through with clients on churches and worship property.

  • Seating capacity and parking
  • Deed restrictions, and zoning outside Houston city limits
  • Fire code occupancy and accessibility for assembly use
  • Condition of the roof, HVAC and other building systems
  • Approvals the congregation or denomination needs to buy or sell
  • Property tax status after the sale
  • Financing from lenders that work with churches
  • Flood plain status and drainage

Questions

Common questions.

Do you help congregations buy or sell church property in Houston?

Yes. We represent churches selling buildings or land, congregations looking for a new home, and buyers of former church property across greater Houston.

Who has to approve the sale of a church?

That is set by how the church is governed: a board vote, a congregational vote, or approval from a denomination or diocese that holds title. We confirm which approvals are needed before the property is listed.

How is church property valued?

There are fewer comparable sales than for most property types, so value is often based on land value, the cost to replace the building, and what the property is worth to other users such as congregations, schools or developers.

Can a church building be used for something else?

Yes. Former churches become schools, childcare centers, event venues and offices, or are redeveloped. The City of Houston has no zoning, but deed restrictions and building codes for the new use still apply, and nearby cities have zoning.

Do churches pay property taxes in Texas?

Property owned by a religious organization and used primarily for worship can qualify for an exemption under Texas law. A buyer that does not qualify will generally pay property taxes, which affects what the property costs to own. The county appraisal district's records show whether a property currently has an exemption.

How do churches finance a building purchase?

Common sources include banks and credit unions that specialize in church lending and denominational loan funds. Lenders usually review the congregation's giving history and financial statements.
