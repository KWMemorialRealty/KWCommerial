  • Raw land and acreage
  • Farm and ranch property
  • Sites for owner-users and build-to-suit projects

Who we work with

  • Landowners selling acreage or development tracts
  • Developers looking for sites for new projects
  • Businesses buying land to build their own facility
  • Investors holding land for future growth
  • Buyers of farm and ranch property
How a deal works

From first call to closing.

What working with us on land and development property typically looks like.

Buying land or a development site

  1. Define the projectIntended use, size, access, utilities, budget and timeline.
  2. Site searchWe identify listed and off-market tracts and screen them for restrictions, access and flood plain.
  3. Contract with a feasibility periodPrice, earnest money and enough time to confirm the site works for your project.
  4. FeasibilitySurvey, title, environmental review, utilities and any MUD, drainage, deed restrictions and zoning.
  5. ClosingWe coordinate title and closing, and connect you with the professionals your project needs next.

Selling land

  1. Pricing and valuation guidanceA broker opinion of value based on comparable sales, location, access, utilities and potential uses.
  2. Best use and best buyersWe identify who will value the site most: developers, owner-users or investors.
  3. MarketingListing on our search and KW Commercial's, and outreach to developers, builders, investors and brokers.
  4. Offers and negotiationPrice, feasibility period, contingencies and closing timeline.
  5. Due diligence and closingWe support the buyer's feasibility work and coordinate the closing.

Questions

Common questions.

Does Houston have zoning?

The City of Houston does not have a zoning ordinance. Land use is shaped instead by deed restrictions and the city's development regulations, and many surrounding cities do have zoning, so each site needs its own review.

What should I check before buying commercial land?

Typical checks include deed restrictions and any zoning, access and frontage, utilities, flood plain status, environmental conditions, and the survey and title.

What is a Municipal Utility District (MUD)?

A MUD is a special district in Texas that finances water, sewer and drainage for development outside city service areas. Property owners in the district pay a MUD tax in addition to other property taxes.

What is due diligence when buying land?

Due diligence is the period after a contract when a buyer investigates the site, typically reviewing the survey, title, environmental reports, utilities, flood plain status and allowed uses.

What is a Phase I environmental site assessment?

It is a report that reviews a property's history and current conditions for signs of contamination. Lenders and buyers often require one before buying commercial land.

How is commercial land priced?

Land is often priced per acre or per square foot, based on comparable sales, location, access, utilities and what can be built on the site.
